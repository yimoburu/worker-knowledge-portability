# Worker Knowledge Portability Charter

**Version:** 0.1 discussion draft

**Status:** Request for critique

**Implementation:** Not authorized

## 1. Purpose

AI-assisted work can produce records that combine employer-confidential knowledge, shared work product, and reusable worker capability. This charter proposes a minimum protection and due-process floor for systems that capture, infer, retain, evaluate, or transfer knowledge arising from that work.

The canonical stress test is worker offboarding.

## 2. Scope and limits

The charter applies to workers, employers, AI vendors, collective bodies, certifiers, reviewers, and regulators involved in knowledge or capability outcomes derived from AI-assisted work.

It does not replace intellectual-property, employment, privacy, trade-secret, contract, or data-protection law. It does not require raw-trace portability, protected-source disclosure, employer endorsement of every worker claim, or one product or business model.

Participation in telemetry or AI-assisted work does not automatically constitute consent to capability portability, secondary use, or an ownership decision.

## 3. Substantive rights

### 3.1 Transparent participation

People must be told what is captured, inferred, retained, shared, and used to evaluate them. The consequences of participating or declining must be understandable before those choices become consequential.

### 3.2 Worker capability agency

Workers must be able to describe and carry safely generalized skills and judgment. Employment or employer custody alone must not convert a person's general capability into exclusively employer-owned knowledge.

### 3.3 Employer knowledge protection

Portability must not expose confidential sources, trade secrets, regulated data, or unlicensed work product. A capability abstraction must not allow a recipient to reconstruct protected knowledge.

### 3.4 Claim-level separation and minimization

A whole trace, conversation, or file must not receive one rights status merely for administrative convenience. Decisions should operate at the smallest practical knowledge unit and disclose only what is necessary.

### 3.5 Separation of claims from evidence

A generalized capability claim may travel while protected evidence remains under employer custody. A challenge may change the claim's trust status or prevent endorsement, but must not silently erase a worker's generalized self-assertion.

### 3.6 Meaningful review without retaliation

Workers and employers must be able to inspect, challenge, and appeal consequential decisions without hidden penalties. Necessary security restrictions must be explicit, proportionate, and reviewable.

## 4. Minimum procedural guarantees

1. **Notice and reasons:** Affected parties receive the proposed decision, purpose, governing policy profile, and a human-understandable explanation.
2. **Traceable evidence and attestations:** Claims identify provenance, derivation, evidence custody, and attestor roles. Self-assertion, employer confirmation, and independent certification remain distinguishable.
3. **Four governed outcomes:** Each knowledge unit resolves to employer-retained, shared or licensed, worker-portable, or blocked or disputed.
4. **Time-bounded review:** Silence does not imply endorsement or release protected evidence, and does not create an indefinite veto over a safely abstracted, visibly unverified worker claim.
5. **Dispute containment:** Mixed evidence remains in protected custody while disputed. Only a non-sensitive abstraction may move, with unresolved status visible.
6. **Independent appeal and remedy:** Either party can seek review by a qualified neutral body. A reviewer may inspect protected evidence confidentially without disclosing it to the worker or receiving system.
7. **Durable history:** Decisions, challenges, licenses, corrections, revocations, and superseding claims remain auditable and are not silently rewritten.

## 5. Governed outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning | Movement rule |
|---|---|---|
| Employer-retained | Confidential sources, regulated data, and protected project knowledge | Evidence remains with the employer |
| Shared or licensed | Knowledge or artifacts with negotiated rights and value | Movement follows explicit purpose, scope, duration, revocation, and value terms |
| Worker-portable | Safely generalized capability claim or independently portable personal content | The claim moves with provenance, rights, trust, and lifecycle metadata |
| Blocked or disputed | Mixed evidence or an unresolved rights conflict | No unilateral evidence movement; claim and evidence statuses remain separate |

## 6. Multi-attestor trust model

A capability claim can contain independently visible statements from several roles:

- **Worker:** authors or accepts the generalized claim.
- **Evidence system:** records provenance and derivation without deciding ownership.
- **Employer:** confirms, declines to endorse, or challenges the claim or evidence relationship.
- **Independent reviewer or certifier:** evaluates evidence, process, or a dispute under an authorized policy profile.
- **Receiving verifier:** decides whether the resulting trust level is sufficient for its purpose.

No party's signature proves universal truth. A conforming process must expose who asserted what, under which policy, with what evidence access, and with what current status.

## 7. Governance and legitimacy

Institutional powers should remain separated:

- A protocol steward maintains future specifications through transparent, multi-stakeholder governance.
- Policy-profile bodies adapt common safeguards to jurisdictions, contracts, and sectors without weakening the minimum floor.
- Independent certifiers test implementations against open conformance suites.
- Neutral adjudicators resolve disputes without also controlling the protocol or implementation.
- Worker collectives, unions, guilds, and data trusts have standing to represent workers and challenge systemic behavior.
- A public precedent registry publishes anonymized decisions and reasoning, never protected evidence.
- Stewards, reviewers, and certifiers disclose funding and relationships that could compromise independence.

The rule maker, implementer, certifier, and adjudicator must not all be the same institution.

## 8. Incentive alignment

- Workers receive understandable capture rules, recognition, continuity, review, and remedy.
- Employers receive protected evidence custody, lower leakage risk, clearer offboarding, and defensible audit records.
- AI vendors receive common interoperability and assurance semantics instead of bespoke bilateral rules.
- Worker collectives receive standing and a mechanism to negotiate policy profiles.
- Auditors and regulators receive inspectable decisions without centralizing all protected evidence.

Basic inspection, correction, dispute, and permitted export rights must not be paywalled. Participation choices must not use deceptive defaults. Shared or licensed outcomes must state purpose, scope, duration, revocation, and value terms.

Success must not be measured only by telemetry volume. Relevant measures include informed adoption, worker and employer trust, protected-information leakage, review latency, dispute patterns, remedy quality, and successful portability.

## 9. Red lines

A process fails this charter if it permits:

- Blanket ownership of an entire trace based only on storage, employment, or tool ownership.
- Leakage of protected knowledge through a supposedly safe abstraction.
- Credential laundering that presents a self-asserted or inferred capability as employer-endorsed or independently certified.
- Permanent veto through delay or non-participation.
- Hidden capture, hidden consequences, or coercive participation defaults.
- One interested party controlling rules, implementation, certification, and appeal.
- Silent deletion or rewriting of disputes, attestations, or claim history.
- Proprietary lock-in presented as interoperability.

## 10. Conformance boundary

Conformance would certify the behavior of a process and its safeguards under standard and adversarial cases. It would not determine universal legal ownership, guarantee that every claim is true, or promise zero risk.

This draft does not yet define a protocol, schema, certification program, adjudicator, or reference implementation.

## 11. Review question

Does this charter describe a real cross-industry gap that cannot be adequately addressed through existing law, contracts, credentials, provenance standards, and data-portability mechanisms? Evidence supporting **continue**, **narrow**, or **stop** is equally valuable.
