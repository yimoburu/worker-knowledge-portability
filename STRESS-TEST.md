# The AI Offboarding Test

## Purpose

Find where this idea fails before asking anyone to support or build it.

## Scenario

A worker asks to carry a generalized record of capabilities developed through AI-assisted work. The former employer must retain confidential evidence. A receiving employer needs to distinguish self-assertion from attestation. Mixed or disputed evidence cannot move unilaterally, but delay cannot silently erase a safely generalized worker claim.

## Questions for critics

1. Which worker right is missing, unsafe, or too difficult to exercise?
2. Which legitimate employer interest remains exposed?
3. Where could a worker, employer, vendor, reviewer, or certifier game the process?
4. Who could legitimately attest, challenge, certify, and hear an appeal?
5. What evidence would justify continuing, narrowing, or stopping this effort?

## Ground rules

- No request to enable telemetry or endorse a product.
- No assumption that employment, custody, or participation determines ownership of an entire trace.
- No requirement for raw-memory portability.
- No implementation commitment.
- Do not disclose confidential workplace examples or personal data.

## Decision after critique

Continue only if a multi-stakeholder group finds a real cross-industry gap and believes the charter adds value beyond existing law, contracts, and standards. Record objections and minority views. The result should be **continue**, **narrow**, or **stop**.
